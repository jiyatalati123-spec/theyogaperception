A Guide on the
Path of Stillness

I came to yoga before I could fully articulate what yoga meant. I was five years old when I first stepped onto the mat — not in a formal class, but with the natural ease of a child for whom movement and breath were already a mother tongue. The asanas became my earliest language, and the mat, my most honest mirror. What began as childhood curiosity deepened steadily into devotion.

For years, that devotion found expression in competition. I took to the stage and earned recognition for my precision, my form, and my command of the body. But something quietly shifted. In the arena, the practice that had once felt like an inward conversation began to feel like a performance outward — something to be judged, compared, won. The asana had become a workout. And I knew, with increasing clarity, that this was not what had called me to the mat as a child. I stepped away from competition — and stepped, with intention, toward teaching. Toward the work in.

Finding my voice as a teacher meant first sitting at the feet of many. I studied under a wide range of teachers, absorbing different lineages, methodologies, and philosophies — classical Hatha, breath-led Vinyasa, the quiet rigour of restorative practice. Each teacher left something with me: a cue, a correction, a question that lingered. Over time, these threads wove themselves into something distinctly my own — an approach that is precise without being rigid, warm without being casual, deeply classical yet wholly present.

My formal studies deepened at The Yoga Institute — one of the world's oldest and most respected centres of yogic learning — where I immersed myself in yoga not merely as physical practice, but as a complete science of living. And yet, for all of this, I hold one truth above all others: I am still a student. The practice is infinite. The learning never ends. It is this humility — rare in a teacher, essential in a guide — that my students feel in every session.

The Practice
ॐ
"Yoga is not a workout.
It is a work in." — A philosophy Jiya lives by
Prāṇāyāma

Breath

Pranayama forms the foundation of every practice. Through conscious breathwork, we awaken the life force within — calming the nervous system, clearing the mind, and preparing the body for deeper exploration.

Āsana

Alignment

Precision in posture is an act of reverence toward the body. Proper alignment prevents injury, deepens awareness, and allows the full expression of each asana — not as performance, but as understanding.

Dhyāna

Stillness

The ultimate purpose of yoga is not performance — it is peace. We cultivate stillness not just in Shavasana, but as a quality of being that follows you long after the mat is rolled away.

Sūrya Namaskāra — The Sun Salutation
12 movements  ·  one breath, one movement  ·  solar energy awakened
